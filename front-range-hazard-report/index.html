03

Nederland, up close

Nederland sits at ~8,200 ft where Middle Boulder Creek meets Barker Reservoir, ringed by dense lodgepole and the Peak-to-Peak corridor — a textbook high-consequence wildland-urban interface with limited, congested egress. The 2016 Cold Springs Fire (≈528 acres, 8 homes lost, ~2,000 evacuated) was the warning shot. The map below opens with USFS wildfire risk-to-structures shading over satellite imagery and the approximate Cold Springs perimeter; pins mark the subdivisions I read as highest-risk.

Why these places. The common thread is forested intermix on steep slopes with single or constrained exits — the conditions that turn a fire into a life-safety crisis. Big Springs, for example, is ~200 residents in thick timber with effectively one way out; the town has been working toward a second evacuation route. Click each pin for the specifics.

07

How this ranking was derived

A data-anchored research synthesis — real federal/state hazard datasets, read together and ordered by judgment, rather than a single engineered score.

01

Hazard intensity from the source rasters. Wildfire weight comes from USFS Wildfire Risk to Communities — chiefly Risk to Potential Structures and Wildfire Hazard Potential (30 m, 2024 vintage). These are the same layers shaded on the maps, so what you see is what drove the call.

02

Flood weight from regulated zones + history. FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer for the regulatory floodplain, cross-checked against the 2013 flood after-action record and active Colorado Hazard Mapping re-studies (e.g. Lefthand Creek).

03

Cross-hazard context from FEMA NRI. The National Risk Index (county + census-tract) anchors relative risk and expected annual loss; it resolves to tract, not parcel, so it sets the baseline rather than the final order.

04

Local plans break the ties. Boulder County's CWPP / fireshed work, municipal WUI ember-cast mapping, the Headwaters CPAW assessment for Nederland, and disaster after-action reports supply the neighborhood-level detail — egress, fuels, structure density — that coarse national data can't.

05

Drought as a multiplier. U.S. Drought Monitor, CWCB, and Western Water Assessment conditions weight current susceptibility — dry fuels and low snowpack push otherwise-moderate areas up.

What this can and can't tell you

  • Granularity gap, narrowed not closed. National datasets resolve to tract; neighborhood pins come from local plans and disaster reports, then matched to the raster. The order is reasoned, not modeled at parcel scale.
  • Boundaries are approximate. Neighborhood pins and historic fire perimeters are hand-placed for orientation. For a regulated answer, click through to the live services.
  • Wildfire is no longer just "the mountains." The 2021 Marshall Fire — Colorado's most destructive — was a wind-driven grassfire in suburban Superior and Louisville. Flat, grassy edges are real WUI.
  • For a parcel-level answer, go to the source tools in §08 — especially CO-WRAP / the Colorado Wildfire Risk Viewer (address-level fire intensity) and Boulder County's floodplain web map.
  • Not insurance, lending, or safety advice. Use this to orient, then verify with official maps and local emergency management before any decision.
